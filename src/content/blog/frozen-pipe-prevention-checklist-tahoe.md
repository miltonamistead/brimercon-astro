Pipes freeze in Tahoe homes every winter, and the damage from a burst pipe dwarfs the effort of preventing one. Work through this checklist before the first hard freeze, and again before leaving a second home empty for the season.

## Know where your main shutoff is

Every item below matters less if nobody can stop the water fast. Find your main shutoff valve now, while nothing is wrong. Make sure every adult in the house knows where it is and that the valve actually turns. Gate valves in older homes can seize after years without use. If yours will not budge, have it replaced before winter. Label it with a tag so a housesitter or guest can find it under stress.

## Insulate the pipes that freeze first in Truckee homes

Pipes in unheated spaces freeze first: crawlspaces, attics, garages, and exterior walls. At Truckee elevations, overnight lows routinely drop into the teens and single digits, so marginal insulation is not enough.

- Wrap exposed supply pipes in unheated areas with foam pipe insulation, sealed at the joints.
- Pay special attention to pipes along exterior walls and in crawlspaces.
- Insulate the crawlspace itself where practical; a cold crawlspace defeats pipe insulation.
- Check that existing insulation has not slipped, compressed, or been chewed by rodents.

## Set the right temperature before you leave Tahoe

Never set the thermostat below 55 degrees when leaving a Tahoe home in winter, even for a weekend. For longer absences, 60 is safer, since rooms far from the thermostat run colder than the hallway where it hangs. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm air reaches those pipes. If your thermostat is programmable, do not use deep setbacks overnight; the few dollars saved are not worth a burst pipe. A Wi-Fi thermostat that alerts you when indoor temperature drops is cheap insurance for a second home.

## Drain outdoor spigots and hose bibs

Outdoor hose bibs are the most common freeze point we see.

- Disconnect all hoses before the first freeze. A connected hose traps water in the bib.
- Shut off the interior valve feeding each hose bib, then open the outside bib to drain it.
- Leave the outside bib open a crack through winter so any residual water has room to expand.
- If a bib lacks an interior shutoff, have a frost-free sillcock installed.

## Winterize a second home the full way

If the home will sit empty for weeks in freezing weather, a checklist is not enough; the plumbing needs a full winterization. That means draining the water heater or putting a tankless unit into bypass, blowing out supply lines, adding antifreeze to drain traps and toilets, and shutting off the main. This is the single highest-value service for a Tahoe second home, and it is worth having done professionally once so you see exactly what it involves. Our winterization page walks through the full process.

## What to do if a pipe freezes in North Lake Tahoe

If a faucet gives you a trickle or nothing on a cold morning, you may have a frozen pipe. Act quickly but gently.

- Keep the faucet open a crack. Flowing water, even a trickle, helps melt ice.
- Warm the frozen section with a hair dryer or warm towels, working from the faucet back toward the coldest area.
- Never use an open flame or heat gun. Too much heat too fast can burst the pipe or start a fire.
- If you cannot find the frozen section, or if a pipe has already burst, shut off the main and call a plumber.
- Do not assume one frozen pipe means the rest are fine. Check every faucet.

## Your printable checklist

Print this page and keep it with your winter supplies.

- Main water shutoff located, tested, and labeled.
- Exposed pipes in crawlspace, attic, and garage insulated.
- Crawlspace vents and access sealed against drafts.
- Thermostat set to at least 55 degrees, 60 for long absences.
- Cabinet doors open under sinks on exterior walls.
- Hoses disconnected; hose bibs shut off inside and drained.
- Second home: full winterization scheduled or completed.
- Wi-Fi thermostat or temperature alarm active for empty homes.
- Every adult in the house knows where the shutoff is.
- Plumber's number saved in your phone before you need it.
