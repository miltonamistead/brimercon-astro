Last week we said to check your heat tape before the first freeze. Here is how to actually do that.

Heat tape is the cable wrapped around the pipes in your crawl space, your garage, or along an exposed water line. It keeps the pipe above freezing when the air around it is not. In Truckee and Tahoe it does a lot of quiet work all winter long. When it quits, most people find out the hard way.

## How to test it

1. Find where it plugs in. Most heat tape runs to a regular outlet in the crawl space or the garage. Check that the outlet actually has power. A tripped GFCI is the single most common reason heat tape "stopped working."
2. Plug it in and wait. Give it ten or fifteen minutes.
3. Feel the cable. It should be warm to the touch, not hot. If the thermostat end is sitting somewhere warm it may not switch on at all, so test it on a cold morning or cool that end down with a bag of ice.
4. Walk the whole run. Warm at the plug does not mean warm at the far end.

## Signs it needs to be replaced

- Cracked, brittle, or chewed outer jacket
- Scorch marks, melted spots, or a burnt smell
- Electrical tape covering a splice or an old repair
- No label, or a label nobody can read anymore
- You do not know how old it is

That last one matters more than people think. Most manufacturers rate heat tape for three to five years. A lot of what we find under houses up here has been down there for fifteen. Old heat tape is a fire risk, not just a freeze risk.

## Two mistakes we see all the time

### Insulation under the tape instead of over it
The heat tape goes directly against the pipe. The insulation goes over the top of both. Wrap the tape on the outside of the insulation and the heat never reaches the pipe at all.

### Overlapping the cable
Do not wrap heat tape back across itself unless the manufacturer says it is rated for that. Overlapped cable builds heat, and that is where fires start.

## Heat tape is a backup, not a fix

Insulate the pipe properly. Seal up the obvious drafts in the crawl space. Then let the heat tape handle the cold snaps. A pipe with heat tape and no insulation is still at risk the moment the power goes out, and up here the power does go out.
