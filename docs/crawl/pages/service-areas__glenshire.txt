Frequently Asked Questions
Who provides water and sewer service in Glenshire?
Water and electric service in Glenshire is provided by Truckee Donner Public Utility District (TDPUD). TDPUD sources 100% groundwater from the Martis Valley basin. Sewer collection is handled by Truckee Sanitary District (TSD), with regional wastewater treatment managed by the Tahoe-Truckee Sanitation Agency (TTSA). Natural gas is provided by Southwest Gas.
Do I need a high-altitude water heater in Glenshire?
Yes. Glenshire sits at approximately 5,900 to 6,000 feet. At that altitude, reduced oxygen levels affect combustion in gas-fired water heaters. Units installed without proper high-altitude adjustments run less efficiently, produce more carbon monoxide, and wear out faster. Most manufacturers offer factory-configured high-altitude models or conversion kits. The cold groundwater supplied by TDPUD adds further stress on heating elements and burners. Brimer sizes and installs water heaters specifically for Glenshire’s elevation and water conditions.
How do I protect my Glenshire home’s plumbing during a cold snap?
Even in a full-time community like Glenshire, hard freezes can compromise exposed pipe runs. Keep heat on and maintain minimum indoor temperatures, especially in areas with plumbing in exterior walls or crawl spaces. Insulate vulnerable pipe runs and let faucets drip on the coldest nights. For long-term protection, heat-trace on exposed lines is the most reliable solution. A Moen Flo system provides continuous monitoring and automatic shutoff if a leak is detected, whether you are home or not. Brimer can assess your home’s specific freeze risks and recommend the right combination of protection.
Do I need a permit for plumbing work in Glenshire?
Glenshire is within the Town of Truckee, so plumbing permits are issued through the Town of Truckee Building and Safety Division. Most plumbing replacements and new installations require a permit. Brimer handles permit filing as part of the project when applicable.
What should I do if my pipes freeze in Glenshire?
If you suspect frozen pipes, turn off the main water supply to prevent flooding if a pipe has cracked. Do not use an open flame or heat gun to thaw pipes, as this can damage the pipe or start a fire. You can apply gentle heat with a hair dryer or space heater directed at the suspected area, or wrap the pipe in towels soaked in warm water. If you cannot locate the frozen section, or if a pipe has already burst, call Brimer. We locate the failure point, perform controlled thawing where safe, and repair or replace damaged sections. For homes with recurring freeze issues, heat-trace on vulnerable pipe runs is the most reliable long-term solution.

Water heater replacement costs depend on the type of unit (tank or tankless), fuel source (natural gas or electric), the complexity of the venting path, and whether the installation requires code upgrades to bring the setup current. A standard tank replacement in a home with existing gas service and a clear venting run is less involved than switching to tankless or upgrading a unit in a home with an older mechanical setup.
