Propane Line Repair in Donner Summit / Serene Lakes
There is no natural gas infrastructure on Donner Summit. All gas-fueled appliances in Serene Lakes run on propane, supplied by individual tanks on each property. Propane lines supply furnaces, water heaters, fireplaces, cooktops, dryers, and outdoor appliances. Over time, connections loosen, fittings corrode, and flex lines deteriorate. At this elevation, propane lines face additional stress from extreme freeze-thaw cycles, heavy snow loading on tank-to-house supply runs, and potential damage from snow removal operations. The 2011 propane line failures along Palisade Road, Baxter Street, and Tamarack Way, caused by extreme snowpack pressure on buried lines, illustrate the kind of risk this community faces.
If you smell propane (a distinct sulfur or rotten-egg odor), evacuate the home immediately, avoid operating switches or electronics, and call your propane supplier and 911 from outside the home. Frequently Asked Questions
Who provides water and sewer service in Serene Lakes?
Water and sewer collection in Serene Lakes is provided by Sierra Lakes County Water District (SLCWD). The district’s primary drinking water source is Lake Serena, with a backup well source. SLCWD serves the roughly 1,038 lots in the Serene Lakes subdivision across a 2,450-acre service area. Sewage is collected by SLCWD and pumped to the Donner Summit Public Utility District (DSPUD) wastewater treatment plant in Soda Springs for processing. There is no natural gas service on Donner Summit. All gas-fueled appliances run on propane. Electricity is provided by PG&E.
Do I need a high-altitude water heater in Serene Lakes?
Yes. Serene Lakes sits at 6,800 to 7,200 feet, among the highest elevations in the Truckee-Tahoe corridor. At that altitude, reduced oxygen levels significantly affect combustion in propane-fired and gas-fired water heaters. Units installed without proper high-altitude adjustments run less efficiently, produce more carbon monoxide, and wear out faster. Because Serene Lakes homes use propane rather than natural gas, the water heater must also be properly configured for propane fuel with the correct orifice sizing and gas pressure regulation. The cold water drawn from Lake Serena enters well below typical temperatures, increasing recovery time and putting additional stress on heating elements and burners. Brimer sizes and installs water heaters specifically for Serene Lakes’ elevation, water conditions, and fuel type.
How do I protect my Serene Lakes home’s plumbing when I’m away?
The most effective approach combines smart leak detection with proper winterization. Propane supply should be verified before winter vacancy to ensure the heating system can maintain minimum temperatures through the season. At Serene Lakes’ elevation, freeze risk extends well beyond the core winter months, with hard freezes possible into late spring and starting again in early fall. Serene Lakes is in unincorporated Placer County, so plumbing permits are issued through the Placer County Building Services Division. Most plumbing replacements and new installations require a permit. Brimer handles permit filing as part of the project when applicable.
What should I do if my pipes freeze in Serene Lakes?
If you suspect frozen pipes, turn off the main water supply to prevent flooding if a pipe has cracked. Do not use an open flame or heat gun to thaw pipes, as this can damage the pipe or start a fire. You can apply gentle heat with a hair dryer or space heater directed at the suspected area, or wrap the pipe in towels soaked in warm water. If you cannot locate the frozen section, or if a pipe has already burst, call Brimer. We locate the failure point, perform controlled thawing where safe, and repair or replace damaged sections. For homes with recurring freeze issues, heat-trace on vulnerable pipe runs is the most reliable long-term solution. At Serene Lakes’ elevation, homes without heat-trace on exposed lines face a higher baseline freeze risk than any other community in the corridor.
