How do I winterize my mountain home's plumbing?
Winterization involves draining water lines and fixtures, adding antifreeze to traps, shutting down the water heater, and setting up monitoring. We offer professional winterization services and can also install smart water monitoring systems for year-round protection.
What should I do if a pipe freezes?
If you suspect a frozen pipe, do not attempt to thaw it with open flame or excessive heat. Turn off the water supply as a precaution and call us at 530-587-0733. We use controlled, professional methods to restore flow safely and prevent the pipe from bursting.
Do you work with property managers?
Absolutely. We regularly coordinate with property managers for seasonal homes, providing winterization, spring startup, emergency response, and ongoing maintenance. We communicate clearly and can work with your preferred scheduling and access arrangements.
How long does a water heater last in a mountain home?
Tank water heaters typically last 8 to 12 years, though hard water and altitude can shorten that. Tankless units often last 15 to 20 years with proper maintenance. Annual inspections help catch issues early and extend the life of your system.
Should I switch from a tank to a tankless water heater?
It depends on your usage patterns, home size, and whether the home is used seasonally. Tankless units offer continuous hot water and easier winterization, but they cost more upfront. We'll assess your situation and give you honest pros and cons for both options.
What is a smart leak shutoff system?
A smart leak shutoff system, like Moen Flo, installs on your main water line and monitors flow, pressure, and temperature continuously. If it detects a leak, unusual usage, or freeze risk, it can shut off your water automatically and alert you via a smartphone app.
Is a smart water monitor worth it for a seasonal home?
Seasonal-use homes are where these systems provide the most value. A leak or freeze event in an unoccupied home can cause tens of thousands of dollars in damage. Smart monitoring gives you 24/7 protection and peace of mind, and may qualify you for insurance discounts.

Can you run a gas line to my outdoor kitchen or fire pit?

How do I know if I have a gas leak?
Common signs include a sulfur or rotten-egg smell, hissing near gas lines, dead vegetation near outdoor lines, or higher-than-expected gas bills. If you suspect a leak, leave the area immediately and call us or your gas utility.

What causes low water pressure in mountain homes?
Common causes include partially closed valves, mineral buildup in supply lines, undersized piping, or pressure regulator issues. We'll diagnose the specific cause and recommend the right fix.
